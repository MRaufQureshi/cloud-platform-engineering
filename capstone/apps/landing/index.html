Parked

01 The idle asset

The biggest battery you own is parked outside.

The average car in Germany is driven 42 minutes a day and spends about 21 hours parked at home. An electric one carries a battery that could run the house for days.

  • 42min driven per day, on average1
  • ~21h parked at home per day, on average1
  • ≈70kWh average battery in a new EV in Europe2
  • ≈7days of a typical 3-person home's electricity3

A 70 kWh battery is nearly seven times the size of the average new home battery (10.3 kWh)4. In practice only part of it is ever lent out.

03 Your rules

Always ready when you are.

You set the departure time, the minimum charge and how much the car may lend. T.H.E.O. is designed to re-plan every quarter of an hour around them, so the car is charged when you need it and works for you when you don't.

  • Driver first

    Your departure time and minimum charge come first. Leaving early? You'll be able to override the plan in one tap.

  • Battery-aware

    Smart charging can reduce wear; lending power adds a little. The plan stays within your carmaker's limits.5

  • Grid-friendly

    Charge when power is cheap and plentiful. Give back when the grid needs it most.

05 Vehicle-to-grid

Now multiply it.

More than 2 million battery-electric cars are registered in Germany6, and most are parked when the grid is under the most pressure. One study estimates that widespread bidirectional charging could save Europe's power system about €22 billion a year by 2040 (scenario)11.

06 Fleets

A depot full of batteries.

Vans and company cars sit plugged in for hours between shifts. T.H.E.O. is designed to lend their power only when the grid is under stress, only from vehicles with at least 20 points to spare, never below their minimum, and never from one that leaves within three hours.

09 Honest answers

Good questions.

Will it wear out my battery?

It adds some cycling. Studies find the effect modest, and smart charging on its own can reduce wear. The plan is designed to stay within your carmaker's limits.5

Will my car be charged when I need it?

That is the design rule: your departure time and minimum charge come first, and you'll be able to override the plan at any time. If a target can't be reached in time, for example after a late plug-in, the app will tell you.

Which cars and chargers work?

Bidirectional charging depends on the car, its software and the wallbox. Support is growing quickly. We'll check your setup before a pilot starts.

What do I need at home?

A car and wallbox that can charge both ways, and a smart meter (needed for dynamic tariffs). Rooftop solar helps, but it is not required.

Is this allowed in Germany?

Yes. Since 2025 every supplier must offer a dynamic tariff7, the “double grid fee” on stored power has been removed8, and the regulator's 2026 rules treat bidirectional chargers much like home batteries9. Some details are still being phased in.

How much will I really save?

It depends on your tariff, car, solar and habits. In our illustrative model, smart charging delivers most of the saving for commuters, and vehicle-to-home matters more when the car is home during the day. Independent analysis finds that vehicle-to-home alone rarely pays for the extra hardware, and that the biggest gains come when the car can also support the grid.5
